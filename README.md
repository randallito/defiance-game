# The Defiance

A strategy game about civil resistance during authoritarian consolidation.

**Status: Beta — for testing only. Likely contains errors.**

## What is this?

The Defiance is a single-player browser game where you lead a movement through 8 turns of strategic decisions. Each turn presents a scenario grounded in real events and civil resistance research, then asks you to diagnose what's happening and choose a response. Your choices ripple forward — the regime adapts to your strategy, institutional pillars shift based on your approach, and early investments (or gaps) shape what's possible in later crises.

The game is a tool for sharpening strategic thinking and sparking conversation. It is not a map of the territory.

## How to play

Open `index.html` in any modern browser. No server, no dependencies, no install.

Or play at: [https://randallito.github.io/defiance-game/](https://randallito.github.io/defiance-game/) *(if GitHub Pages is enabled)*

## Game mechanics

- **8 turns** across 3 phases: The Consolidation (2025), The Resistance Builds (2025-26), The 2026 Midterms
- **6 institutional pillars** to shift: Business & Markets, Security & Military, Religious Institutions, Media & Press, Labor & Unions, Civil Servants & Bureaucracy
- **5 movement metrics**: movement power, popular support, organizational capacity, coalition unity, democracy
- **Consequence chains**: early choices create flags that unlock (or close off) possibilities in later turns
- **Regime AI**: the regime reacts to your strongest metrics and exploits your weakest
- **Concept Codex**: 19 unlockable entries teaching civil resistance frameworks as you encounter them in gameplay
- **Multiple endings** determined by accumulated choices

## Key research sources

The game draws on civil resistance research and case studies from dozens of countries. Key sources include:

- Chenoweth & Stephan — *Why Civil Resistance Works* (2011)
- Tufekci — *Twitter and Tear Gas* (2017)
- Pinckney & Trilling — "Breaking Down Pillars of Support for Democratic Backsliding" (2024)
- M. Gessen — *Surviving Autocracy* (2020); NYT reporting (2025-26)
- Magyar — *Post-Communist Mafia State* (2016)
- Snyder — *On Tyranny* (2017)
- Batura — "How to Compete in Unfair Elections" (2024)
- The Hold the Line Guide
- Protect Democracy — Executive Override framework

Full source citations appear in-game alongside each scenario and codex entry.

## Technical details

The entire game is a single HTML file (~190KB) with inline CSS and JavaScript. No frameworks, no build step, no external dependencies. Game state is held in memory and resets on reload.
